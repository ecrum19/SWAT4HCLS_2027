\begin{abstract}
% Context: Why the need is so pressing or important
Connecting genomic observations to biomedical knowledge depends on preserving the context that makes each observation interpretable.
In the Variant Call Format (VCF), overlooking version rules, field declarations, or sample-specific allele relationships can silently associate evidence with the wrong variant.
% Need: Why something needed to be done at all
Existing semantic models leave parts of this context to application-specific conventions, limiting what users can retrieve and integrate without the loss of context.
% Task: What was undertaken to address the need
We developed VCF Core, a vocabulary that makes this context explicit and provides version-specific representations for VCF 4.1--4.5, allowing data to be interpreted according to their declared specification.
% Object: What the present document does or covers
This paper evaluates VCF Core's specification conformance, robustness, and practical utility through four research questions.
Our reproducible workflow combines competency questions, specification-derived tests, negative controls, cross-implementation comparisons, and semantic round-tripping, with explicit expected answers and transparent reporting of passing, failing, and untested requirements.
% Findings: What the work done yielded or revealed
The specification-derived assessment identifies and demonstrates both preservation and direct structural retrieval in all 210 expanded-profile cases, exercising 53 of 94 registered specification requirements.
Cross-implementation testing exposed a silent allele-association error missed by a pre-existing VCF-RDF converter's tests and guided its correction.
A synthetic integration experiment further demonstrates that standard SPARQL queries can correctly join local-allele evidence to external annotations while retaining source provenance, without application-specific decoding.
% Conclusion: What the findings mean for the audience
These findings show how explicit VCF semantics make operations otherwise dependent on format-specific code available through native graph relationships, providing a tested foundation for reusable conversion and querying.
% Perspectives: What the future holds, beyond this work
The accompanying workflow can be adapted to other specification-based vocabularies, while independent testing and application to biomedical datasets can extend the evidence for VCF Core and guide its adoption.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}

VCF Core provides a shared semantic foundation for representing VCF observations together with the context needed to interpret them.
The assessment demonstrates structured retrieval for the tested requirements, reuse across two separately implemented producers, and an allele-dependent integration task with checked answers and source provenance.
The remaining gaps concern untested requirements, incomplete converter support, and evaluation beyond synthetic examples.
By making declarations, allele relationships, and sample context explicit, the vocabulary allows conversion engines and downstream queries to reuse the same interpretation rules while connecting source observations to biomedical models.
We invite independent review and testing to extend the evidence for this resource and clarify where further development is needed.
